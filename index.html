🔬 The Dark Store Problem: Mathematical Formulation

In modern quick-commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart), order fulfillment times are strictly capped at 8 to 10 minutes. Over 65% of the operational warehouse cycle is spent by human pickers walking through aisles. Dark Store Blitz models this high-frequency optimization problem using constrained graph theory and discrete mathematics.

1. The Traveling Salesperson & Order-Batching Heuristic

Given a dark store layout modeled as an undirected or directed graph $G = (V, E)$, the packing station is fixed at $v_0 = \text{A3}$. For an incoming customer order ticket requesting an SKU set $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_k\} \subset V$, the picker's objective is to find a minimum-distance Eulerian path starting and ending at $v_0$:

$$\min \sum_{i=0}^{k} d(v_{\pi(i)}, v_{\pi(i+1)}) \quad \text{where } v_{\pi(0)} = v_{\pi(k+1)} = v_0, \quad \pi \in \text{Perm}(1 \dots k)$$ $$\text{Subject to: } \quad e = (u, v) \in E_{\text{legal}}(t), \quad v \notin V_{\text{blocked}}(t)$$

2. Storage Slotting Constraints & Penalty Functions

In Phase 1, participants design the 24-SKU warehouse layout subject to real-world industrial constraints:

  • Cold-Chain Integrity ($-75\text{ pts}$): Perishables (Cornetto, Frozen Peas, Curd) placed outside the dedicated bottom refrigeration row (Row J1–J3) trigger melting penalties.
  • Chemical Segregation ($-50\text{ pts}$): Household cleaners (Harpic, Surf Excel, Mosquito Refill) placed in the same aisle or adjacent to edible groceries cause cross-contamination violations.
  • Ergonomic Weight Limits ($-30\text{ pts}$): Heavy bulk items like Atta 5kg placed on Top shelves violate warehouse worker safety laws.
  • Fragility Crushing ($-30\text{ pts}$): Heavy goods placed on the same shelving stack directly above Eggs or Bread.

3. Dynamic Disruptions & Resilient Logistics

In Stage 3, the environment becomes non-stationary to test dynamic real-time adaptation:

  • Oil Spills (Directional Flow Constraints): Aisle 6 becomes a one-way directed edge $(B6 \rightarrow I6)$. Pickers cannot backtrack and must route around the warehouse.
  • Fallen Pallets (Topological Obstacles): Key walkway squares ($B4, B5$) are dynamically removed from the accessible vertex set ($V \setminus V_{\text{blocked}}$), forcing longer detour routes.
  • Double Drop (Multi-Order Batching): Teams choose between making two independent runs or using a heavy handcart that increases total effective meters by $\times 1.25$.

4. Production Engineering Stack

  • Core Engine: Pure Vanilla JavaScript with zero heavy dependencies, achieving 60 FPS on low-power mobile devices.
  • Real-Time Synchronization: Server-Sent Events (SSE) `/events` broadcast loop with automatic jittered exponential backoff.
  • Zero-Lag Verification: Mathematical validation runs deterministically on the server to prevent route spoofing or client-side tampering.
